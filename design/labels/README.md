# Jar labels — Eris Jar

Print artwork for the 120 ml Eris Jar, generated by
`scripts/make-labels.py`. Everything is drawn at true millimetre size, so an
SVG placed at 100% is the finished label.

```bash
python3 scripts/make-labels.py                      # SVGs
python3 scripts/make-labels.py --previews           # + PNGs with guides
python3 scripts/make-labels.py --net "NET WT 3.8 OZ (108 g)"
```

The blend names, ingredient declarations and allergen lines are read out of
`data/products.ts`, and the wordmark out of `components/wordmark.tsx`. Edit
those and re-run; do not edit the SVGs by hand or the jar and the website will
start disagreeing with each other.

## The pieces

| File | Size | Goes on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `lid-<blend>.svg` | 65 mm circle | the flat top of the lid |
| `front-<blend>.svg` | 190 × 38 mm | the front of the glass |

The jar is 76.5 mm across and 51 mm tall, so the glass is 240.3 mm around. The
front label is a rectangle rather than a wrap: at 190 mm it covers about four
fifths of the way round and leaves 50 mm of bare glass at the back. Wider
looks better and wraps further; `FRONT_WIDTH` at the top of the script is the
dial. The `--previews` PNGs draw trim guides that the SVGs do not carry.

Three columns under a centred wordmark, after the reference label: what the
product is in the middle, what is in it on the left, what to do with it on the
right. The side columns hold different amounts of text per blend, so each one
sets itself at the largest size that still clears the bottom edge.

Add bleed to taste when you order. Most printers want 1.5–3 mm on a die-cut
circle; ask yours before scaling anything.

## Three things to settle before printing

**The lid diameter is a guess.** The spec sheet gives the glass as 76.5 mm and
the thread as "special", which says nothing about the flat top of the closure.
65 mm is conservative for a jar this size. Measure a real lid, then change
`LID_DIAMETER` at the top of the script — every other number on the lid label
is derived from it.

**Net contents is blank on purpose.** The jar holds 120 ml, which is 4.06 US
fluid ounces, but a balm is labelled by weight and whipping beats air into it.
Nobody knows what a filled jar weighs until one is filled and weighed. Do that,
then pass `--net`. US law requires the net quantity on the principal display
panel, so this is not optional, and guessing it is a weights-and-measures
problem rather than a design one.

**The contact line is half placeholder.** `ORIGIN` and `CONTACT` at the top of
the script give "Made in Cave Creek, AZ", `@yoursocialhandle` and
`sixskincareproducts.com`. The name and place of business of the manufacturer,
packer or distributor is required on the label, and a street address is
required unless the firm is listed in a current telephone or city directory.
Check that Six Skincare Products LLC is, or add the street.

## Why the copy reads the way it does

Three lines from the reference label are not reproduced here, for the same
reasons the website copy was written the way it was:

- **"Sustainably sourced"** — an unqualified environmental claim, which the
  FTC Green Guides treat as a claim about the whole product and its packaging.
  "Sustainable" came off the homepage for this reason.
- **"optimized for deep skin repair"** — repairing skin is a structure or
  function claim. A cosmetic that claims to repair is an unapproved drug in
  the FDA's reading, and it is the single riskiest sentence on that label.
- **"replenish skin"** — same family, milder. The BENEFITS block here says
  what the base is and how it feels instead, using the wording already
  approved for the site.

What is kept: "rich in vitamins A, D, E and K" survives as *tallow naturally
carries* those vitamins — a statement about the material, not a promise about
skin, which is how it is phrased on the website too.

- The origin line is qualified — an unqualified "Made in the USA" is an FTC
  claim, and the shea butter and the essential oils are imported. The front
  label says where it is made rather than making the claim.
- The label states what the product is, what is in it, how much of it there is
  and who made it. It makes no claim about what it does, which is what keeps a
  cosmetic a cosmetic.
- The ingredient declaration is set in common names, with the botanical names
  stripped. This is worth knowing: 21 CFR 701.3 asks for INCI names, and for a
  plant material the INCI name is the Latin binomial — "Butyrospermum Parkii
  (Shea) Butter" rather than "shea butter" — so a common-name declaration is
  not a compliant one. The full declaration is on every product page either
  way. `plain()` in the script is the one function to delete to put the
  binomials back on the jar.
- The naturally occurring fragrance allergens are published on each product
  page but are not on the label. No US regulator asks for them here. If these
  are ever sold into the EU or UK, they have to go back on — the script still
  reads them out of `data/products.ts` if you re-add the block.
- There is no lot or best-by field on the face. Small makers usually ink-jet
  or sticker those on the base, which is easier than reprinting labels.

## Fonts

The text is live, not outlined, in a Palatino / Georgia / serif and a
Helvetica / Arial stack — faces any print shop already has. The wordmark is
already vector outlines, so Cloister Black is not needed to render it. If your
printer would rather have everything as paths, say so and the script can
outline the rest too.

## The printed batch, and what the next one needs

The first run of labels was printed before this artwork existed: white type
on the dark amber jar, SIX in Cloister Black, the blend name in a script,
`Net Wt. 25g ℮` at the foot of the front panel. It looks considerably better
than anything generated here, and the artwork in this folder is sized for a
different jar entirely. Treat these files as superseded until they are
rebuilt to match.

Two things to correct on the next print:

- **The net quantity needs both units.** US cosmetic labelling requires the
  declaration in US customary *and* metric. `Net Wt. 25g` carries only
  metric. It should read `NET WT 0.88 OZ (25 g)`.
- **Drop the ℮.** That is the European estimated-quantity mark. It has no
  standing in the US and means nothing to a US inspector or customer.

Two rules worth checking while the artwork is open: the net quantity has to
sit within the bottom 30% of the front panel, and the type has to be at least
1/16 inch tall. The placement is right on the printed batch; the type size is
close enough to the limit to be worth measuring.

Nothing here is urgent for a first small run. Net quantity is enforced by
state weights-and-measures officials working retail shelves, not by anyone
looking at a maker's first few dozen jars, and the two things that would
have been expensive — a drug claim on the label, an incomplete ingredient
declaration — are both correct. Fix it on the reprint.
