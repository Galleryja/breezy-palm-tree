# Jar labels — Eris Jar

Print artwork for the 120 ml Eris Jar, generated by
`scripts/make-labels.py`. Everything is drawn at true millimetre size, so an
SVG placed at 100% is the finished label.

```bash
python3 scripts/make-labels.py                      # SVGs
python3 scripts/make-labels.py --previews           # + PNGs with guides
python3 scripts/make-labels.py --net "NET WT 3.8 OZ (108 g)"
```

The blend names, ingredient declarations and allergen lines are read out of
`data/products.ts`, and the wordmark out of `components/wordmark.tsx`. Edit
those and re-run; do not edit the SVGs by hand or the jar and the website will
start disagreeing with each other.

## The pieces

| File | Size | Goes on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `lid-<blend>.svg` | 65 mm circle | the flat top of the lid |
| `body-<blend>.svg` | 244.3 × 32 mm | around the glass |

The jar is 76.5 mm across and 51 mm tall, so the glass is 240.3 mm around. The
body label is that plus 4 mm of overlap; the last 4 mm are left blank because
the leading edge covers them. The `--previews` PNGs shade that strip and draw
the lid's trim circle — the SVGs themselves carry no guides.

Add bleed to taste when you order. Most printers want 1.5–3 mm on a die-cut
circle; ask yours before scaling anything.

## Three things to settle before printing

**The lid diameter is a guess.** The spec sheet gives the glass as 76.5 mm and
the thread as "special", which says nothing about the flat top of the closure.
65 mm is conservative for a jar this size. Measure a real lid, then change
`LID_DIAMETER` at the top of the script — every other number on the lid label
is derived from it.

**Net contents is blank on purpose.** The jar holds 120 ml, which is 4.06 US
fluid ounces, but a balm is labelled by weight and whipping beats air into it.
Nobody knows what a filled jar weighs until one is filled and weighed. Do that,
then pass `--net`. US law requires the net quantity on the principal display
panel, so this is not optional, and guessing it is a weights-and-measures
problem rather than a design one.

**The address is a placeholder.** `BUSINESS` in the script carries
`[street address]` and `[city, state, ZIP]`. The name and place of business of
the manufacturer, packer or distributor is required on the label.

## Why the copy reads the way it does

- The origin line is qualified — "Made in the USA with domestic and imported
  ingredients". An unqualified "Made in the USA" is an FTC claim, and the shea
  butter and the essential oils are imported.
- The label states what the product is, what is in it, how much of it there is
  and who made it. It makes no claim about what it does, which is what keeps a
  cosmetic a cosmetic.
- Allergens are carried on the label as well as the website. No US regulator
  asks for them.

## Fonts

The text is live, not outlined, in a Palatino / Georgia / serif and a
Helvetica / Arial stack — faces any print shop already has. The wordmark is
already vector outlines, so Cloister Black is not needed to render it. If your
printer would rather have everything as paths, say so and the script can
outline the rest too.

## The size on the website does not match this jar

`data/products.ts` lists every balm as `6 oz`. This jar holds 120 ml — about
4 fl oz of volume, and less than that by weight once it is whipped. One of the
two has to change before launch.
