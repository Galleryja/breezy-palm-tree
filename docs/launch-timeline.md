# Launch timeline

Three tracks running at once: **production** (making a sellable jar),
**development** (the site), **sales** (getting it in front of people). Weeks
are counted from today, not from calendar dates, because two of the three
depend on lead times nobody has quoted yet.

## What this assumes

Correct these and the weeks move:

- The formula is settled. Four blends, two formats, no reformulating.
- Jars are **not** ordered yet — the Eris Jar spec sheet is a quote.
- There is no product photography.
- There is no live Stripe key and no live domain.
- Six Skincare Products LLC exists, based in Cave Creek, AZ.

## The critical path

Most of the work below can happen in any order. Three things cannot:

```
fill a jar → weigh it → print labels → sellable product
photography → finished product pages → launch
jar order placed → jars arrive (2–4 weeks, unquoted) → fill → everything else
```

**Order the jars first.** They have the longest lead time and nothing else
about the physical product can finish without them. The labels cannot be
printed until a filled jar has been weighed, and the weight depends on which
jar. Everything on the production track queues behind that one purchase order.

## Weeks 0–2 · decisions that unblock the rest

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Lock the jar sizes | The catalogue says `6 oz`, the Eris Jar holds 120 ml (~4 oz), the reference label says 2 oz. Three different numbers. Pick, then the site, the labels and the pricing all follow. |
| Order jars and lids | Longest lead time in the project. Ask the supplier for the lid's flat-top diameter while ordering — the lid label die depends on it. |
| Fill one jar and weigh it | Whipped tallow has air beaten into it; the net weight cannot be calculated, only measured. Blocks label printing. |
| Settle the label's address | Name and place of business is required. A street address is required unless the LLC is listed in a current city or telephone directory. |
| Register the social handle | `@yoursocialhandle` is printed on the label. Whatever goes on the jar has to exist and stay yours. |

## Weeks 2–6 · production

- **Three test batches** at the final formula. Record the weight of each fill.
  Consistent fills are what makes a printed net weight honest.
- **Order labels** as soon as the net weight is known. Usually 1–2 weeks.
- **Retain samples** from batch one. Tallow balm has no water in it, so
  microbial growth is not the main risk — **oxidation is**. Open a retained
  jar at 30, 60 and 90 days and smell it. That is what sets the best-by date.
  Twelve months is the usual claim for a tallow balm; confirm it rather than
  copying it.
- **Lot coding.** Ink-jet or sticker the lot and best-by on the base, so the
  printed labels stay usable across batches.
- **Product liability insurance** before the first sale, not after it.
- **FDA obligations under MoCRA.** Facility registration and product listing
  have a small-business exemption tied to average annual gross sales, and a
  whipped balm is not in the excluded categories — so the exemption probably
  applies here. Safety substantiation, compliant labelling and adverse-event
  records apply either way. **Verify this against current FDA guidance or a
  cosmetics compliance consultant.** It is the one item on this list where
  being wrong is expensive, and it is not something to take from me.

## Weeks 2–6 · development (in parallel)

- **Fix the catalogue** to match the real jars — sizes, prices, cost per unit.
- **Rewrite the four roller descriptions.** They are still placeholder text I
  wrote during the build; the four balm descriptions are yours.
- **Product photography.** Eight SKUs, roughly three shots each. This is the
  single biggest blocker on this track and the easiest to underestimate. The
  site currently draws a generated jar as a stand-in.
- **Stripe live key**, then a full test: test card first, then one real
  transaction of your own, then refund it.
- **Shipping rates.** The checkout collects addresses for US/CA/GB with no
  rates attached. Flat rate is fine to start; decide before launch.
- **Policy pages** — returns, shipping, privacy, terms. Expected by customers
  and by payment processors.
- **Deploy and domain.** The URL printed on the label must resolve on the day
  the first jar ships.

## Weeks 6–8 · soft launch

Twenty jars to people who will tell you the truth. What you want back is
photography you can use, three sentences of review copy, and confirmation
that the balm survives a week in a hot car in Arizona. Fix what that turns up
before spending on a full run.

## Weeks 8–12 · sales

- **Own site** — the whole build is pointed at this.
- **Instagram** — the handle on the label needs something behind it.
- **Local markets.** Cave Creek and Scottsdale both have them, and a squat
  jar of tallow balm sells far better when someone can smell it.
- **Wholesale** to one or two local boutiques, once you know the unit cost
  well enough to survive a 50% wholesale margin.

Set a reorder trigger at **40% of the first run sold**, not at the last jar.
Jar lead times do not shorten because you are out of stock.

## Where this slips

Jar lead time, photography, and a label reprint because the net weight was
estimated instead of measured. The first is out of your hands; the other two
are the reason the order above is what it is.
